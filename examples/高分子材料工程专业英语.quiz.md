**10. Radical chain polymerization consists of three steps: initiation, propagation and termination. Initiation involves two reactions: the homolytic dissociation of an initiator I to give a pair of radicals R·, and the addition of this radical to the first monomer molecule to form the chain-initiating species M·. Propagation is the growth of M· by successive additions of monomer molecules; each addition regenerates a radical of the same identity but larger by one monomer unit, and kp for most monomers is 10^2 to 10^4 L/(mol·s). Termination is the annihilation of radical centres by bimolecular reaction between two radicals, either by combination (coupling) or, more rarely, by disproportionation, and kt is typically 10^6 to 10^8 L/(mol·s). The chain nature resides in the propagation step: large numbers of monomer molecules are converted to polymer for each initial radical produced in initiation. Termination is fast but does not prevent propagation because radical concentrations are very low (about 10^-8 M) and the rate depends on only the one-half power of kt.**　*描述题 · 5 分 · 应用 · 中等*
　　采分点：initiation（引发）；propagation（增长）；termination（终止）；combination (coupling)（偶合）；disproportionation（歧化）
　　关键在最后一句：链式特征由增长步决定，而非引发步。终止有两种方式（偶合/歧化），漏写歧化会丢分。
　　> 原文依据：Radical chain polymerization is a chain reaction consisting of a sequence of three steps: initiation, propagation, and termination.
　　> 出处：UNIT 2 · 书页 8-9（阅读材料）

**11. Polymerization is a chain reaction in two senses: kinetically, because one activation step sets off a sequence in which monomer molecules are added one after another (2000 to 20000 monomers per second) until the active state is terminated; and in terms of product, because the reaction product itself is a chain molecule whose length is proportional to the kinetic chain length. Initiators are commonly called polymerization catalysts because very small amounts of them produce a large amount of polymer, in ratios of 1:1000 to 1:10000, which superficially resembles catalysis. Strictly, however, they are not true catalysts: the initiator enters the reaction as a real partner and can be found chemically bound in the reaction product, i.e. in the polymer molecule, whereas a true catalyst is recovered unchanged at the end of the reaction.**　*问答题 · 5 分 · 分析 · 困难*

**20. In A—A + B—B polymerization the chain grows only by reaction between the two complementary functional groups, so the maximum attainable degree of polymerization is governed by the ratio of the two monomers. Any imbalance means one kind of end group runs out while the other remains, and growth stops there; high molecular weight therefore requires an exact stoichiometric balance. If a monofunctional impurity is present, its single functional group reacts with a chain end and renders that end inactive, capping the chain. It acts as a chain stopper, limiting the molecular weight regardless of conversion. For the same reason high-purity monomers are needed in A—B polycondensation, and only high-yield reactions are practical, since side reactions upset the stoichiometric balance.
